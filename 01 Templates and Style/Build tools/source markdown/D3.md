

**Checklist 1 - The Initiation Night**

*For the Personal Mentor (with the Lodge Mentor, DC and Tyler). Customs vary between Lodges - adapt to your own.*

<table>
<colgroup>
<col style="width: 22%" />
<col style="width: 78%" />
</colgroup>
<tbody>
<tr class="odd">
<td><strong>In the days before</strong></td>
<td><p>☐ Draft Festive Board speech provided well in advance, for him to adapt and make his own</p>
<p>☐ Dress, timing, venue, parking or lift arrangements all confirmed</p>
<p>☐ A quiet word about practicalities: comfortable trousers (one leg will be rolled up), reading glasses in his pocket if he needs them, cash for the alms collection and raffle</p>
<p>☐ Any adjustments confirmed discreetly - mobility, kneeling, faith requirements, the Volume of the Sacred Law appropriate to him (via the DC and Secretary)</p>
<p>☐ Contact him the day before, or on the morning: “We're thinking of you. Arrive early. You'll be guided at every step.”</p></td>
</tr>
<tr class="even">
<td><strong>On arrival</strong></td>
<td><p>☐ Meet him at the door - he should never stand alone in the anteroom</p>
<p>☐ Introduce him to the Tyler and the Junior Deacon, who will look after him during the ceremony</p>
<p>☐ Introduce him to the Worshipful Master and DC if opportunity allows</p>
<p>☐ Reassure: everyone in the room has done this; the ceremony is symbolic, safe and dignified</p></td>
</tr>
<tr class="odd">
<td><strong>During the meeting</strong></td>
<td><p>☐ Sit as close to the Initiate as custom allows once he is admitted; quiet explanation and reassurance as events unfold</p>
<p>☐ Ensure someone is with him at every point he is outside the Lodge room</p></td>
</tr>
<tr class="even">
<td><strong>The Festive Board</strong></td>
<td><p>☐ Explain Masonic Fire and the order of toasts before the meal - never let him struggle in front of the room</p>
<p>☐ Ensure he is seated according to Lodge custom and never left among strangers</p>
<p>☐ Be ready to support his response to his toast - he has the draft speech; steady him beforehand</p></td>
</tr>
<tr class="odd">
<td><strong>End of the evening</strong></td>
<td><p>☐ Congratulate him warmly; make sure others do the same</p>
<p>☐ Confirm the next Lodge date and your first one-to-one meeting - book it now, don't leave it open</p>
<p>☐ Present or signpost the Initiate's Guide and any Lodge welcome material, without overloading him</p></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

*PREPARE runs through everything on this page: there should never be any surprises. The small human details - the trousers, the glasses, the cash - are everything.*



**Checklist 2 - His Second Meeting**

*The neglected danger point: a new Brother is treated as the hero of his Initiation, then too easily forgotten. Not in Hertfordshire.*

<table>
<colgroup>
<col style="width: 22%" />
<col style="width: 78%" />
</colgroup>
<tbody>
<tr class="odd">
<td><strong>Before the meeting</strong></td>
<td><p>☐ Contact him in the days before: confirm he is coming, answer any questions the first month has raised</p>
<p>☐ Confirm the summons has reached him and decode it together - what each item means, how to reply, dining arrangements</p>
<p>☐ Regalia and dress checked - gloves, apron arrangements per Lodge custom</p></td>
</tr>
<tr class="even">
<td><strong>On the night</strong></td>
<td><p>☐ Meet him on arrival again - the anteroom is still an unfamiliar place</p>
<p>☐ Practise the salute and step quietly in a side room before the meeting if he wishes</p>
<p>☐ Ensure he has the words for any odes or hymns the Lodge uses</p>
<p>☐ Sit with him or ensure a briefed Brother does; explain business items as they happen (Risings, ballots, apologies)</p>
<p>☐ If the Lodge works a higher degree, ensure an experienced Brother retires with him and uses the time well</p></td>
</tr>
<tr class="odd">
<td><strong>The Festive Board</strong></td>
<td><p>☐ Seat him with company that will draw him in - do not let established groups close around him</p>
<p>☐ Introduce him to two or three members he has not yet properly met, by name and interest</p></td>
</tr>
<tr class="even">
<td><strong>Afterwards</strong></td>
<td><p>☐ Walk out with him; confirm the next contact and the next date</p>
<p>☐ Note anything that seemed to puzzle or unsettle him - cover it at your next one-to-one</p></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

*SUPPORT is the principle under test at the second meeting. Isolation is one of the earliest predictors of disengagement - our task is to dispel strangeness quickly, through warmth, explanation and genuine friendship.*



**Checklist 3 - Before and After his Passing**

*The Second Degree deserves better than to be treated as a corridor between the First and Third.*

<table>
<colgroup>
<col style="width: 22%" />
<col style="width: 78%" />
</colgroup>
<tbody>
<tr class="odd">
<td><strong>In the weeks before</strong></td>
<td><p>☐ Test questions learned at his pace - rehearse together until he is comfortable, never rushed to fit the Lodge calendar</p>
<p>☐ Explain the tradition: he is tested on competence before advancement, and the questions are also a chance to discuss what the First Degree meant</p>
<p>☐ Explain timing, format and general structure of the ceremony - never ritual detail</p>
<p>☐ Reassure about answering in open Lodge: everyone finds it daunting; rehearsal is the antidote</p></td>
</tr>
<tr class="even">
<td><strong>On the day</strong></td>
<td><p>☐ Contact him the day before or on the morning</p>
<p>☐ Meet him on arrival; re-introduce the Tyler, Inner Guard and Junior Deacon</p></td>
</tr>
<tr class="odd">
<td><strong>Soon after</strong></td>
<td><p>☐ Meet within days: “What did you make of it?” - let him talk first</p>
<p>☐ Explain why he was not hoodwinked this time: he was already a member, proved by his answers</p>
<p>☐ Discuss the themes of the Second Degree - knowledge, reason, the Liberal Arts and Sciences - and their everyday relevance</p>
<p>☐ Ensure he can give the Signs, Token and Word of a Fellow Craft according to the Lodge's working</p>
<p>☐ Present and review the Fellow Craft's Guide</p>
<p>☐ Encourage him to witness a Passing at another Lodge - offer to go with him</p>
<p>☐ Begin the test questions for his Raising, at his pace</p></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

*Readiness, not convenience: if he is not ready to be passed, the right response is to wait - and to fill the time well. What he will remember of a rushed ceremony is not the ceremony; it is the anxiety.*



**Checklist 4 - Before and After his Raising**

*The most significant of the three degrees - and the gateway to the highest-risk period for losing a member.*

<table>
<colgroup>
<col style="width: 22%" />
<col style="width: 78%" />
</colgroup>
<tbody>
<tr class="odd">
<td><strong>In the weeks before</strong></td>
<td><p>☐ Test questions rehearsed and comfortable</p>
<p>☐ Prepare him practically: timing, format, general structure - and advise him to arrive well rested and having eaten</p>
<p>☐ Prepare him honestly for the ceremony's weight: it is powerful, unlike what has come before, and entirely symbolic, safe and dignified</p></td>
</tr>
<tr class="even">
<td><strong>On the day</strong></td>
<td><p>☐ Contact him the day before or on the morning</p>
<p>☐ Meet him on arrival; re-introduce those who will guide him on the night</p></td>
</tr>
<tr class="odd">
<td><strong>Soon after</strong></td>
<td><p>☐ Check in within days of the ceremony - the reflective conversation matters more here than after any other degree</p>
<p>☐ Discuss the central teaching of the Third Degree at his pace: mortality, integrity, the life well lived</p>
<p>☐ Ensure he can give the Grip and the Five Points of Fellowship according to the Lodge's working</p>
<p>☐ Cover visiting: how to sign in, Visitors' Toast etiquette, respect for other Lodges' workings</p>
<p>☐ Explain the structure of Grand Lodge and Provincial Grand Lodge and their principal Rulers</p>
<p>☐ Present and review the Master Mason's Guide</p></td>
</tr>
<tr class="even">
<td><strong>The months after</strong></td>
<td><p>☐ This is the danger window - many losses occur after the Third Degree, when the visible journey seems complete. Show him the road ahead: LoI, visiting, Provincial and Light Blue events, areas of personal interest</p>
<p>☐ In due course, and at his pace: the Royal Arch as the completion of the journey - information, not recruitment; the decision and its timing are his alone</p>
<p>☐ Watch for any dip in enthusiasm now the ceremonies have finished - see the Warning Signs card</p></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

*The Third Degree is not the summit; it is the point where the real journey begins. ENLIGHTEN carries the load from here.*



**Checklist 5 - His First Lodge of Instruction**

*The LoI is the backbone of Lodge culture - and the first visit can underwhelm if nobody manages expectations.*

<table>
<colgroup>
<col style="width: 22%" />
<col style="width: 78%" />
</colgroup>
<tbody>
<tr class="odd">
<td><strong>Before he goes</strong></td>
<td><p>☐ Explain what LoI actually is: a relaxed rehearsal - officers in different seats, corrections, restarts, laughter. Unexpectedly ordinary, and that is its charm</p>
<p>☐ Check what the evening will hold: if there is little for an Entered Apprentice, tell him honestly - “come anyway and I'll introduce you to a few people” - or steer him to a better week</p>
<p>☐ Confirm time, place, dress (usually informal - check your LoI's custom) and any cost</p></td>
</tr>
<tr class="even">
<td><strong>On the night</strong></td>
<td><p>☐ Go with him, or arrange a named Brother to meet him at the door</p>
<p>☐ Introduce him to the Preceptor and explain the Preceptor's role</p>
<p>☐ No pressure to take work - attendance with the option not to perform is explicitly fine</p>
<p>☐ Involve him in something small if he is willing: following the book, a walk-through part</p></td>
</tr>
<tr class="odd">
<td><strong>Afterwards</strong></td>
<td><p>☐ Ask what he made of it; normalise anything that surprised him</p>
<p>☐ Encourage a regular pattern of attendance without turning it into an obligation - if he misses a week, the only right response on his return is warmth</p>
<p>☐ Never let LoI absence be met with jokes or pointed remarks - that culture loses members</p></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

*A new member's first experience of an acrimonious or unwelcoming LoI can affect his participation permanently. Make it fun - people attend, and remain, because they enjoy it.*
