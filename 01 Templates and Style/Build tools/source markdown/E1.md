*Tier E - training and development*

1\. Purpose and Format

Every newly appointed Lodge Mentor in Hertfordshire receives an induction within three months of appointment - ideally before it. Its aims: that he leaves knowing exactly what the role is (and is not); that he has met the Provincial team and his fellow Lodge Mentors; that he can find and use every tool in the library; and that he has heard, in person, the philosophy the Province expects him to embody.

- **Format:** a single evening of about two hours, delivered by an Assistant Provincial Grand Mentor (or the Deputy/Provincial Grand Mentor), in person where numbers allow or via Teams. Run whenever three or more new Lodge Mentors are awaiting induction; individual induction by an APGMen where waiting would leave a Lodge unsupported.

- **Materials:** Lodge Mentor's Guide; Personal Mentor's Guide; Personal Mentoring Plan; Mentor Meeting Guide; Stage Checklists; Warning Signs card; Retention, Retrieval and Migration Guide; The Hertfordshire Mentoring Principles (one per attendee, printed); the F3 Lodge presentation for them to take away and deliver in their own Lodge.

- **Follow-up:** the new Lodge Mentor is added to the monthly mentoring call and to DAWG; his APGMen makes contact within a month of the induction; the appointment is recorded on the Provincial register.

3\. The Charge to the Newly Appointed Lodge Mentor

*To be presented in writing to every new Lodge Mentor on appointment, and read at the induction. It complements - and never replaces - the presentation and explanation of the Mentor's Jewel in open Lodge.*

Brother, the office you have accepted is unlike any other in the Lodge. The Treasurer keeps the accounts and the Secretary keeps the record; you keep something less visible and more easily lost - the experience of every member, from the man who has not yet been Initiated to the Past Master wondering what his Freemasonry is now for.

Your duty is not to mentor every member yourself. It is to make sure that every member is mentored: that each new Brother has a Personal Mentor chosen with care; that those mentors are supported, encouraged and thanked; that no one's absence goes unnoticed and no one's question goes unasked; and that the Brethren of your Lodge come to understand that mentoring is not an office but a culture - that we are all mentors to others in Freemasonry.

Four words describe the work: Prepare, Guide, Support, Enlighten. Prepare every member for what lies ahead of him, so that there are never any surprises. Guide him with accurate and honest information, so that he navigates an unfamiliar world with confidence. Support him through whatever life and the Lodge may bring, asking always 'How can we help?' and never 'Why weren't you there?' And enlighten him - patiently, at his pace, following his curiosity and not your own - so that Freemasonry becomes to him not information learned but a life enjoyed and understood.

Remember the order of things: his family first, his work second, his Masonry third. Hold that line for him even when the Lodge's programme tempts otherwise, and you will keep men the calendar would have lost.

You do not hold this office alone. Behind you stand the mentors of every Lodge in this Province, the Provincial Mentoring Team, and guidance for every stage of the road. Ask for help early and often; in Hertfordshire, asking is a strength.

And from your first day, look for the Brother who will one day succeed you - for the measure of your work is not what mentoring looks like in your Lodge during your tenure, but what it looks like after it.

*May the work be patient and purposeful - and may Freemasonry flourish, root and branch.*

Presented on behalf of the Provincial Grand Mentor, Province of Hertfordshire.
